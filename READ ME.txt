GNOMESHELL
==========

Mold Shell is now called GnomeShell. Your scans and settings stay where
they were, in Documents\Mold Shells. After unzipping this version, run
"Make desktop shortcut.bat" again: it makes the new GnomeShell shortcut
and removes the old Mold Shell one. If you pinned Mold Shell to the
taskbar, unpin it and pin GnomeShell instead.

Builds the 3D-printed shell (mother mold) for a latex mold scan: a box with
a 1/4 in wall, split by flat cuts so every piece pulls straight off, with
45 degree bungee channels at the corners, an optional pour collar, and thin
test slices to print first.

You need: Windows with Blender 5.2 installed. Nothing else.

START
  1. Unzip this folder somewhere it can stay (for example Documents\GnomeShell app).
  2. Double-click "Make desktop shortcut.bat" once.
  3. Double-click the GnomeShell shortcut. GnomeShell opens in its own
     window (it uses Microsoft Edge to draw the screen, so it needs no
     other install). A loading screen shows straight away while Blender
     starts, then turns into the app. It opens full screen: F11 switches between full
     screen and a normal window, and Alt+F4 closes it. Closing the window
     quits GnomeShell.
     If Blender isn't found, put the path to blender.exe in blender_path.txt.
     If something goes wrong, the engine's log is %TEMP%\GnomeShell.log.
  4. After unzipping a new version, close every GnomeShell window first.
     If an older copy is still running, the new one asks it to quit; if a
     window says it's talking to an older copy, close it, end blender.exe in
     Task Manager if it's there, and start GnomeShell again.

UNITS
  Settings > Show sizes in: mm, cm or inches. Every size you see or type
  is in that unit; the files are always in mm.

PHONE SCANS (the GnomeShell Scan app)
  Turn an object into a scan with your Android phone's camera. The phone
  takes the photos; this PC builds the 3D model from them and opens it here
  like any other scan, at its real size and standing upright.

  Once:
  1. Install GnomeShell Scan on the phone (GnomeShellScan.apk; see
     android\README.md in this folder).
  2. Print the scan sheet: Load scan screen > Phone scans > Print the scan
     sheet. Print at Actual size (100%), not Fit to page. The bar along the
     bottom should measure exactly 100 mm.
  3. Open GnomeShell, then open the app. It finds the PC by itself when both
     are on the same Wi-Fi. Type in the 6-digit code that the Phone scans
     panel shows. The first time, Windows may ask whether Blender can use the
     network: choose Allow (Private networks).

  Each scan:
  1. Lay the sheet flat in good, even light (daylight from a window, no harsh
     shadows). Stand the object on the grey ring, arrow pointing away.
     Something too big for the sheet can stand next to it instead, as long as
     the sheet shows in most photos.
  2. In the app press New scan and walk slowly round the object. The dial
     shows three rings: low (looking across at it), middle and high (looking
     down). With Auto on, a photo is taken each time you hold still over a
     slice that has none yet. Fill all three rings: about 50 photos. Blurry
     photos are thrown away and you're asked to take that one again.
  3. Press Done, then Send to GnomeShell. The photos go to the PC and the
     model starts building on its own (Phone scans shows how far along it is;
     the app does too). Keep GnomeShell open until it's done.
  4. Press Open in GnomeShell, then carry on as with any scan.

  Building takes a while on the processor: roughly 10 to 30 minutes for 50
  photos, more on Fine detail. The first scan also downloads the two free
  programs that do the work (COLMAP and OpenMVS, about 150 MB, kept in
  %LOCALAPPDATA%\GnomeShell\photoscan-tools).

  Tips: shiny, see-through or plain single-colour objects scan badly (dull
  them with a light dusting of talc or dry shampoo). Don't move the object
  or the sheet during a scan. Fill the picture with the object and sheet.

  Each phone scan is kept in Documents\Mold Shells\Phone scans, with its
  photos, the finished model.stl and a log.txt that says what happened.

UPDATES
  When GnomeShell opens it checks github.com/physare/gnomeshell for a newer
  version. If there is one, a card at the bottom says what's new: Update
  downloads it, puts it in this folder and restarts GnomeShell (your scans
  and settings are kept); Ignore skips that version until the next one.

USE
  1. Drop a latex scan onto the window (or pick a recent one). Later, File
     at the top left (or Ctrl+O) opens another scan or a recent one. STL, OBJ,
     PLY and .blend files all open. From a .blend, every visible object
     comes in as it looks in Blender (modifiers applied), joined into one
     model. If the .blend won't open, export an STL or OBJ from Blender.
     A scan that comes in under 20 mm across was saved in other units
     (Blender's meters, say), so GnomeShell scales it up by 10, 100 or
     1000 to somewhere between 50 mm and 1 m and tells you. If the size
     it picked is wrong, type the real one under Size.
  2. Prepare & cut:
       - The tool rail down the left of the view has the view buttons
         (Front, Side, Top, Fit), undo and redo, Pieces and Lines, Insert
         view and ? for the shortcuts. The panel on the right has four tabs,
         one job each: Scan, Cuts, Insert and Settings. Under each tab's name
         is a short summary, and the keys 1 to 4 switch tabs. Clicking a
         piece or a cut in the view goes to the Cuts tab by itself.
       - Scan tab: set the size, lay it flat, rotate, fix units, trim a
         ragged base, repair holes, or simplify a heavy scan so builds go
         faster.
       - Size: measure the latex and type the width, depth or height in mm
         (or a scale %), then press Apply or Enter. With Keep proportions on,
         one number sets the other two; turn it off to set each one. The
         cuts move with the scan. The usual way to get the fit right: press
         Test slices only, print them, see how they fit on the latex, nudge
         the size, cut them again. The size you set is kept for that scan, and
         Reset scan goes back to the scan as it was loaded.
         You can also size it by dragging: on the Scan tab the latex has a
         gold box round it. Drag a corner to scale the whole scan (Shift for
         5% steps, Esc to put it back). With Keep proportions off, white dots
         on the sides and top stretch it one way only. Letting go resizes it
         the same as Apply.
       - Base: puts a base on the model itself (the pour collar only
         changes the shell). Drag the model in, open the Scan tab, and set
         the Height (or drag the blue dot under the model); the base shows in
         blue. Follow the bottom fills in under the model right up to its
         underside, however uneven or tipped that is, and carries it straight
         down; Flat slab is a plinth round the whole footprint, Margin wider,
         which is better when the model stands on feet. Flare widens it
         toward the bottom. Widen grows it out all round. If only a paw or a
         corner touches the floor, Reach up is raised for you (the panel says
         so) so the base meets the rest of the underside; a chin or a raised
         paw higher up is left alone. Move it to change that. Press Add base
         to put it on. Save model STL adds a base you haven't added yet, then
         saves the model as it is now as "<name> - model.stl" in
         the scan's folder and opens that folder; Show saved model in folder
         opens it again later. Reset scan takes the base off again.
       - Fit your printer (Scan tab): pick your printer on the Settings tab
         (Bambu A2L, H2S, H2D, X1/P1/P2S/A1, A1 mini, Prusa, Creality,
         Elegoo, or Custom with width, depth and height). Its space shows as
         a box round the model, red when the model is too big. Suggest cuts
         finds level cuts (and upright ones if it's too wide) so every part
         fits, at spots where the cut is solid with room for pegs. Each part
         shows in its own color on the model, and each cut has a numbered
         orange handle: drag it to move that cut, or use its slider or type
         a number. + Level cut, + Upright, across and + Upright, front-back
         add a cut. An upright cut can go through the whole model or just
         one level (the picker under it); Suggest cuts only cuts the level
         that's too wide, where the cut is solid, so you don't get thin
         slivers. A part thinner than 20 mm is flagged in the list. Split
         and save parts
         saves "<name> - part 1.stl" and so on in the scan's folder, with
         one peg on the part below each cut and a matching hole in the part
         above (one per separate bit of the cut). Peg diameter, length and hole clearance are set there too.
         Draw a cut: press Free line, Box or Ellipse, then drag on the view
         round the area you want as a part of its own, like a selection in
         a photo editor. The cut goes straight through the model the way
         you're looking (turn the view first), and gets one peg on the part
         inside pointing out into a hole in the part round it. A free line
         must be one loop that doesn't cross itself. Esc cancels; the x on
         the cut's row removes it.
         Pull the parts apart (under the cuts) spreads the model's parts
         out in the view so you can see how they go together; the shell
         isn't moved. When you let go of the slider the parts are cut for
         real (not saved yet), so they show solid with their pegs and
         holes. Moving a cut puts the slider back to 0.
         When you build, a shell piece too big for the printer is split the
         same way (SHELL_1A, SHELL_1B...), with pegs where the wall is thick
         enough. A cut through a curved wall can leave separate bits; each
         one is joined back onto the part beside it when that still fits,
         so every part prints in one piece. The same goes for a mold piece
         that would come out in separate bits: a small bit joins the piece
         next to it, a big one becomes a piece of its own. Untick "Split shell pieces that don't fit the printer" on
         the Settings tab to stop that.
       - Pour spout (Scan tab): set the Diameter and Height, pick Straight up
         or Out from the surface, then Place by clicking (click the model
         where it goes) or At the top. It shows in green; Add spout joins it
         onto the model. The shell is left open where the spout comes out,
         so you can pour through it. Reset scan takes it off.
       - Seam card (Scan tab): a thin card (0.56 mm) that stands straight
         out from the model, its inner edge shaped to sit against it, from
         the bottom up. Latex over it to get a flap that opens so the skin
         comes off. Place by clicking, then click the model where the card's
         top should be (click again for another card), or Suggest a spot:
         it checks whether the skin has to stretch over something bigger
         round than the narrowest part below it, and if so puts a card up
         the plainest side. Each card's top has a slider. Sticks out (50 mm),
         Thickness, Gap to model (0 fits exactly) and a Stiff edge along the
         outer edge are settings. Save seam card STL saves each card laid
         flat as "<name> - seam card.stl" in the scan's folder. Cards show
         in the view; untick Seam cards in the Show menu at the top to hide
         them.
       - Gap webs (Scan tab): a thin flat sheet across a gap that's closed
         all round, like between the legs or an arm on the hip, so the
         latex can be pulled out and the web punched out of the concrete.
         Find gaps looks for them; Click a gap, then click it in the view,
         fills it at the angle that covers the most of the gap. Each web has
         Turn and Tilt sliders; Thickness (1 mm) is shared. It shows in blue;
         Add webs joins them onto the model and Ctrl+Z takes them off. A gap
         has to be see-through from some angle to be found.
       - Cuts: the best first cut is found for you. Each piece shows in its
         own color on the model; red areas would catch. Click the model to
         pick a piece. Only the cut you're working on shows its plane (Lines
         mode shows them all).
         Click a cut's card to open it. Move it with Turn / Tilt / Position,
         type an exact number in the box beside each slider, or drag the
         handle in the view: the arrow slides the cut, the two rings turn and
         tilt it. Hold Shift while dragging to snap to 5 degrees or 5%.
         The overhang number in the bottom right of the view updates as you
         go, and shows what your last change did (for example 2.3 lower). The target button
         beside each slider finds the setting with the least overhang;
         "Optimize all three" tries them together. "Cut through the whole
         shell" adds a cut that splits every piece it crosses.
       - Draw a cut: pick the pencil in the tool rail (or press D) and drag
         a line across the model. The cut goes through that line straight
         away from you, so turn the view until you see the seam edge-on
         first (Front, Side or Top). It only goes through the pieces the
         line crosses; a line across the whole shell cuts everything.
         Hold Shift to keep the line level, upright or at 45 degrees. Then
         slide or turn it with its handle like any other cut. P goes back
         to picking.
       - Slice it for me: lays out every cut in one go. It tries cut 1 with
         the piece that has the most red split again and again, and thin
         layers (side to side, front to back, level, and each half of
         cut 1 in layers), each layer kept whole or split its own way, then
         keeps the one with the least overhang for the fewest pieces. One
         more piece has to save about 0.4% of overhang to be worth it. No
         piece is smaller than Smallest piece (7% of the shell unless you
         change it under Settings, Printer & cutting), and there are never
         more than Suggest up to. "Other layouts" below the button lists
         the best layout for each number of pieces, so you can pick fewer
         or more pieces in one click. Your old cuts are replaced; Ctrl+Z
         brings them back.
       - Lines: pick Lines in the tool rail, click a line between two
         pieces (Shift-click for more) and press Delete. Only that part of
         the cut goes, and the two pieces beside it become one. That is how
         you keep the top whole while the bottom stays divided. Double-click
         a line to pick its whole cut. Or Shift-click pieces and press M
         (or Delete) to join them.
       - Mirror: open a cut and press Mirror (or right-click it) to copy it to
         the other side, left <-> right or front <-> back as you're looking
         at the model. The copy goes in the matching piece on the other side
         and its card says "mirror of" the cut it came from.
       - Piece numbers show in the view. Click a number to pick that piece.
       - Right-click a cut, a line or a piece for its options.
       - Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y redoes (or the arrow buttons in the tool
         rail). Press ? in the view for every shortcut.
       - Pieces: click a piece (in the list or the view), or Shift-click
         several (Ctrl+A picks every piece), then split them Left | right,
         Front | back or Top | bottom, as you're looking at the model, into
         2 or more parts. One plane runs through all the picked pieces, so
         picking both halves splits both. Pieces side by side in the split's
         direction each get their own cut through their own middle. A cut
         made this way stops at the picked pieces' edges, so a level cut can
         run across the bottom columns only. Each piece gets its own pull direction (the
         arrow); drag the arrow's tip to change it. Each piece shows its
         own overhang and when it comes off ("off 1st", "off 2nd").
       - Saved layouts (at the bottom of the Cuts tab): name the cuts you have and Save. Load one from the
         list any time (Ctrl+Z goes back). The layout you're working on is
         also kept as you go and comes back when you open the scan again.
       - Suggest more cuts keeps your cuts and keeps splitting whichever
         piece has the most red, while a cut saves enough overhang, up to
         the number of pieces set under Printer & cutting and never below
         Smallest piece. What it adds are normal cuts you can move or delete.
       - Pour collar: carries the base opening down so you have room to pour.
       - Test slice: one slice through the whole mold. Every piece it
         crosses gets its own slice there, so together they go all the way
         round the model. Pink in the view shows where it's cut, with a line
         where it meets the latex (untick Test slices in the Show menu at
         the top to hide it). Upright: across the main seam through the
         tallest point of the latex. Level: Level slice height up the shell,
         clear of the channels. Drag the pink dot to move it. "Put the slice
         back where it starts" (Settings tab, Test slice) undoes the move,
         and so does Ctrl+Z.
       - Insert tab (for big pieces, off until you tick it for a scan):
         a hollow silicone insert hangs in the latex so the casting comes out
         with a cavity. Pick round, oval, square or rectangle, and set its
         size, height, silicone wall (3 mm) and the least concrete you want
         round it (20 mm). Ticking it centers the insert on the opening and
         sizes it to the latex, and with "Keep it sized to the latex" ticked
         it sizes itself again whenever you change the wall, concrete, shape
         or center. If the opening is too narrow for your least concrete, it
         keeps your wall and fits with the most concrete the opening allows
         (shown in amber). To size it yourself, pull the dots in the view:
         white on the side for width (and depth), white at the far end for
         height, gold at the opening to slide it. "Fit to the latex" puts
         it back. Ctrl+Z undoes insert changes too.
         "Insert view" (in the tab or the tool rail, or the I key) shows just the insert and its holder,
         solid, with the latex and shell grayed out; the dots still work
         there. Esc goes back to the cuts. It shows
         in blue, with the thinnest spot of concrete marked, and the panel
         says how much silicone to mix. The holder (purple lines) sits on the
         shell's open face with a peg in each corner. The corners are
         numbered 1 to 4 at the pegs, the same numbers the panel's notes use.
         The Bottom view (next to Top, or Ctrl+Numpad 7) looks up from
         underneath, to check the pegs in their slots after a build.
         The pegs always sit at the box's four corners, so the holder is a
         rectangle. Peg size is the width of each peg's head (20 mm by
         default, 10 at the least). If a corner's slot comes too close to the
         latex, press "Fit the pegs" for the biggest pegs that fit all four.
         A rectangle or oval insert, kept sized to the latex, fits its width
         and depth to the opening on their own, so it keeps its shape.
         Taper narrows the insert toward its far end, by that many degrees on
         every side, so it pulls out of the concrete more easily (1 to 3 is
         plenty; 0 keeps the sides straight). The cup and the holder's core
         follow it. Build shell then makes:
           holder.3mf      core (the inside of the silicone) with 3 air
                           holes, hub, arms, four twist-lock pegs and a
                           grooved tab off each corner for a bungee cord
           insert_cup.3mf  the insert mold: a thin skin (Mold wall, 0.56 mm by
                           default) with a copy of the holder's core inside
           and the shell gets a twist-lock slot in each corner of its open face.
         To cast: pour the silicone into the insert mold, to the brim. Once
         it has set, tear the thin wall off, pull the insert off the mold's
         core, and slide it onto the holder's core (the same shape). To use: drop
         the pegs into the slots and turn the holder clockwise (the panel
         says how far, about 20 degrees, as you look down at the opening).
         The last bit of the turn gets stiff as the seat pulls the holder
         down tight, then it clicks: that's locked. Run a bungee over each
         corner tab so the cord sits in its groove and holds the holder down
         while you pour. "Make just the
         holder and cup" makes those two without the shell. If the insert
         pokes through the latex, GnomeShell won't build until it's smaller.
       - Settings tab: pour collar, shell, bungee channels (tick Pairs
         for two side by side at each level), test slice, build plate.
         Anything you change is saved as your default for every scan. Each
         one has a range, and a number outside it is refused with a note.
         "Reset to the defaults" puts them back.
       - Shell style (Settings tab, Shell): Box is the solid box with
         bungee channels. Hug the latex makes a shell that follows the
         latex instead, Thickness thick (4 mm by default), with a raised
         strip along each seam, a U notch at the strip tips every Notch
         spacing for the bungee cord to wrap round the whole mold, a flat
         Foot round the bottom so it stands, and Alignment bumps on the
         strips so the pieces line up. Far less filament and print time.
         The silicone insert needs the Box style for now.
  TABS: every scan you open gets a tab under the menus, like a browser's,
     so you can have several on the go. Click a tab (or Ctrl+Tab and
     Ctrl+Shift+Tab) to go to it: it comes back the way you left it, with
     its cuts, view, step and build. + (or Ctrl+T) is a new tab for
     opening another scan; a scan that's already open just goes to its tab.
     The X on a tab (or Ctrl+W, or a middle click) closes it after asking;
     the scan, its cuts and its files stay, under Recent scans. The tabs you
     had open come back the next time GnomeShell starts.
     Builds take turns: one builds at a time, in the background, and the
     rest wait in a queue and start by themselves. You can keep working,
     in that tab or another, while they do. A tab that's building shows a
     spinner (an empty ring while it waits); a green dot means its build
     finished while you were somewhere else. The bar over the view has
     Stop (or Take it out, while it's waiting). A build uses the scan and
     cuts as they were when you pressed Build, so changing them after
     doesn't change that build.
  3. Test slices only cuts just the slices, in a fraction of the time, so
     you can check the fit before printing the shell. They're exactly the
     slices the full build makes. It clears the scan's folder first, so an
     old shell that no longer matches isn't left lying there.
     Build shell makes everything. Files go to Documents\Mold Shells\<scan name>\:
       test_slices.3mf + SHELL_n_TEST.stl   print these first to check the fit
       shell.3mf + SHELL_n.stl              the shell pieces; shell.3mf is one
                                            Bambu Studio project with every
                                            plate in it
       plate 1.3mf, plate 2.3mf, ...        the pieces plate by plate, when
                                            they take more than one plate
       holder.3mf, insert_cup.3mf           only with the silicone insert on
       <scan> - seam card.stl               only with seam cards placed
       preview_with_latex.3mf               everything together, for looking
     The 3MFs come laid out on the plate, each part flat and apart from the
     others, ready to slice. The Plates view (top left, after a build) shows
     every printed part, each with its name over it, set out on plates the
     size of your printer, as many plates as it takes; each plate is saved
     as its own 3MF. The shell pieces (and every part a big piece was split
     into), the insert holder, the insert mold and any seam cards share the
     plates; the test slices get plates of their own (test_slices.3mf), since
     they're printed first. The insert mold shows with the silicone in it,
     as it would look poured, and after a build the shell view shows the
     holder and the silicone insert in place and the mold beside the shell.
     A part too big even on its own shows in red on a plate by itself.
     Clean up scan (Scan tab): a phone or photo scan often comes back with
     the floor or table it stood on, and other things that were round it.
     Clean up finds the floor, cuts the scan just above it, takes away
     everything not joined to the latex mold and closes it flat underneath.
     When a scan comes in with a floor, a banner offers it. Ctrl+Z puts the
     scan back.
     Paint mask (Mask tab, or press 5): a thin cover that fits over the
     piece with openings to spray through, say to paint the eyes white
     after the whole piece is gray. Turn the view to face the area, draw
     round it (an Ellipse, a Box or a Free line; Shift makes a circle or a
     square) with Paint on, then pick Rub out and draw round each opening.
     Whatever you can see inside the outline is painted or rubbed out; the
     Brush touches up by hand. Ctrl+Z steps back. Build paint mask saves
     "<scan> - paint mask.3mf" and .stl in the scan's folder, dome up and
     ready to slice (it needs supports under the dome). Thickness is 1.2 mm
     and the gap off the model 0.1 mm, so it fits snug; round the openings
     it thins to a fine edge for a crisp paint line. Tape it on to spray.
     If the scan is of the latex mold rather than the piece, set the gap
     to minus the latex's thickness so the mask fits the cast. What you
     paint is kept with the scan.
     A scan with holes in it can't be built: press Repair mesh first. After
     a build, any piece that didn't come out closed is named in red.

Bambu Studio says "invalid config, load geometry data only" for any 3MF it
didn't save itself. Click OK; it's harmless.
